# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Mid-to-senior software engineers with a system design interview approaching — typically within days, not weeks. They're time-pressured, technically competent, and motivated by career outcomes. They open this under pressure and need to leave feeling prepared, not just informed. Secondary: CS students building foundational knowledge at a slower pace, who benefit from the same depth.

## Product Purpose

System Design Lab teaches architecture concepts through three lenses simultaneously: real-world analogies (intuition), live animated diagrams (visualization), and CTO-level interview framing (application). The goal is not passive reading — it's internalizing patterns well enough to explain them under pressure. Success looks like a user who could confidently whiteboard any of the 11 concepts in a live interview.

## Brand Personality

Sharp, technical, empowering. The tone of a senior engineer who explains things clearly without dumbing them down. No hand-holding, no cheerful edtech enthusiasm — just signal, no noise. Confidence is the product.

## Anti-references

- Coursera / Udemy / LinkedIn Learning: pastel edtech warmth, progress bars as emotional motivation, generic "keep going!" energy
- Documentation dumps: walls of text with no interaction or visual anchoring
- SaaS landing page clichés: hero-metric templates, gradient text on every heading, identical card grids
- Bootcamp aesthetics: overly colorful, playful, designed for beginners who need encouragement

## Design Principles

1. **Signal over noise.** Every element earns its place. If it doesn't help the user understand or retain a concept, it's out.
2. **Confidence by design.** The interface should feel like a tool built for experts, not a tutorial for beginners. Density and directness are features.
3. **Comprehension before completion.** Diagrams, analogies, and interview framing exist to build mental models — not to check boxes. Depth over breadth.
4. **The interface gets out of the way.** Navigation, chrome, and scaffolding should be invisible. The concept being studied is the only thing on stage.
5. **Dark is the right choice.** Engineers using this at 11pm before an interview should feel at home, not blinded.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum. Color is never the sole carrier of meaning — diagrams and status indicators must work without color differentiation. Support reduced-motion preference via `prefers-reduced-motion`. Keyboard navigation for all interactive elements.
